# Translation glossary — German → English

The agreed English rendering for every recurring German term on the site.
**Apply these consistently**; if a term here appears in copy you are writing,
use the "Use" column, not a synonym that reads better in that one sentence.
Consistency matters more than local elegance here — a corporate HR reader is
scanning for the same words across pages.

Anything you had to decide that is *not* covered here belongs in
[`TRANSLATION-OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](./TRANSLATION-OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) for David to
confirm.

## Keep in German

Statute and standard names stay German — translating them makes them
unfindable for anyone who needs to look them up. Gloss once on first use, then
use the short form.

| Term | First use | Thereafter |
|---|---|---|
| ASiG | the German Occupational Safety Act (ASiG) | ASiG |
| DGUV Vorschrift 2 | DGUV Regulation 2 | DGUV Vorschrift 2 |
| ArbMedVV | the German Ordinance on Occupational Health Care (ArbMedVV) | ArbMedVV |
| TfV (§ 16) | the German Train Driver Licensing Ordinance (TfV) | TfV |
| EBO | the German Railway Construction and Operating Regulations (EBO) | EBO |
| VDV-Schrift 714 | VDV Publication 714 | VDV 714 |
| Bayerische Landesärztekammer | Bavarian State Chamber of Physicians | — |
| G20 … G46, Asbest | keep the code verbatim (G20, G42, …) | — |

**G-codes are never translated.** They are the legal designation employers
search for. "G42 – Infektionsgefährdung" becomes "G42 – Infection risk", but
the `G42` stays.

## The hardest call: *Arbeitsmedizinische Vorsorge*

**Settled by the client (Aug 2026): "preventive occupational health care".**

There is no clean English equivalent, and it is the single most repeated concept
on the site. Three candidates were live:

- **"screening"** — readable and searchable, but implies looking *for* disease.
  Vorsorge is broader: advice, prevention, and only sometimes examination. This
  was the original draft's choice and has been reversed.
- **"health surveillance"** — the UK/HSE term of art and the closest legal match,
  but it sounds like monitoring employees, which reads badly to an employer.
- **"preventive occupational health care"** — accurate and unambiguous, at the
  cost of being long. **Chosen.**

Where the full phrase would repeat inside one paragraph, **"preventive health
care"** is the accepted short form. Never shorten it to "care" alone, and never
reintroduce "screening" — the site no longer explains that word anywhere.

Note the knock-on effect: *Pflicht-/Angebots-/Wunschvorsorge* became
**mandatory / optional / elective health care**, replacing the earlier
"mandatory / offered / requested".

## Register and style

- Address the reader as **"you"**, the company as **"your company"**. The
  German is formal *Sie*; English marketing copy should not read stiff.
- Keep sentences short. The German copy is already plain; do not add formality
  that isn't there.
- **Sentence case for every heading**, h1 through tile titles, matrix cells and
  button labels: "Occupational health and safety from a single provider", not
  "Occupational Health and Safety from a Single Provider". This matches the
  German side, so the two languages look like one site. Capitals survive only
  for proper nouns (MonacoMed, Munich, Deutsche Bahn), statute names and their
  glossed English forms (DGUV Vorschrift 2, the German Railway Construction and
  Operating Regulations), G-codes, and the first word after a dash or colon.
- Use **British spelling** (organised, centre, programme) — the audience is
  European.
- Numerals and units stay as-is (48 hours, 1–49 employees).
- Keep the em-dash rhythm of the German where it reads naturally in English.
